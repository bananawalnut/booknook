## Security replacement in progress

The first preview, `v0.1.0-preview.20261114`, was returned to draft on November 14; all 17 assets and its tag are preserved. Its archives are not recommended for new installations. [The publication record](public-preview-publication-evidence.json) retains the initial publication and current withdrawal status.

[RUSTSEC-2026-0365](https://rustsec.org/advisories/RUSTSEC-2026-0365.html) identifies uncontrolled peer IdList allocation under `std` in the first preview's `negentropy 0.5.1`. The [maintainer fix](https://github.com/nostrdevkit/negentropy/pull/14) landed in 0.5.2; the selected 0.5.3 also includes a later reconciliation correctness fix. A bounded local parser regression intercepted oversized old-version allocations before forwarding them; fixed initiator/responder cases returned `ParseEndsPrematurely`, with a 544-byte maximum observed allocation. This was not an actual out-of-memory crash or relay exploit test. The reference app uses `fetch_events`/`send_event`, and NIP-77 parser reachability has not been demonstrated.

A fresh official RustSec audit broadened the hold: the workspace lock reported **11 vulnerability findings plus seven informational findings**, including **three unsound findings**; the relay probe reported **six plus five**. [Post-resolution checks](security-dependency-audit-evidence.json) against official database `4ef2df8` now find zero matching known vulnerabilities or unsound advisories in both exact locks. All 358 distinct registry versions are present, checksum-matched and not yanked. Root maintenance notices remain for `basic-toml` and `smallstr`; the probe retains `smallstr`. They are visible, without a waiver or blanket security assurance.

The maintained Nostr SDK **0.45.6** removes the unmaintained relay pool and permits **lru 0.18.2**, requiring migration of exposed Rust Nostr types and custom transport APIs. The app and all SDK packages therefore target **0.2.0**, build **2026111403**, and replacement tag `v0.2.0-preview.20261114`. Fresh Rust/Swift/WASM consumers, all-three Release builds, Swift/app/Rust/npm packages, exact-source CI and fresh public consumers remain pending. No replacement release has been created or published. The held private `0.1.1` candidates and successful source `130d5dba` CI are pre-broader-audit history, not publication qualification. ## Current reading desk

Book and notebook remain visible side by side, with independent scrolling, a resizable divider and one notebook-left/right button. The desk uses matching warm paper and green ink. Notes are distinct text entries separated by quiet rules. Each has a light margin for adding a character card, note options and flipping to its own drawing board. Finger or Pencil draws on the iPad board; two fingers pan and pinch zooms. The board expands around a pan/zoom viewport; returning to text preserves prose, original ink, creation context and note order. Add note starts another entry. Original handwriting and reviewed transcription remain independently recoverable.

Completed notebook and reader ink is checkpointed to a private local journal before asynchronous final persistence. Acknowledged captures recover after process termination, including unresolved reader placement and failed saves. Recovery retains original bytes, source context, parents and retry IDs. Ambiguous reader marks need an explicit retry or unplaced save before portable export. Transcription can be canceled; late recognition results cannot replace a canceled or edited draft. First meaningful text/drawing input captures its available reading context before asynchronous saving; an automatically opened empty note does not invent a creation time. Drawing captures retain their own original time and ebook/audio locations through retry and recovery.
